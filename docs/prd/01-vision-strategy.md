# 00 · Vision & Strategy

## What BudgetKatta is
> **BudgetKatta = India's first Marathi-first AI Financial Operating System.**

Not a finance website. Not a calculator site. Not a blog. Not an affiliate comparison portal. Those are *features inside* the OS, not the product.

Just as:
- **Zerodha** = Investing OS
- **CRED** = Credit OS
- **PhonePe** = Payments OS

…**BudgetKatta = Personal Finance OS (Marathi-first).** The whole of a person's money life — profile, history, documents, goals, health, life events — lives in one system that **remembers, tracks, reminds, measures, and improves** over time, and gets smarter with every interaction.

## Founder vision (canonical)
> *"Forget that BudgetKatta is a finance website. Design it as India's first Marathi-first AI Financial Operating System. The core product is not calculators or affiliate comparisons but a lifelong financial operating system that stores a user's financial profile, remembers history, tracks life events, monitors financial health, manages goals, stores documents, generates monthly AI reports, recommends contextual financial actions, and becomes smarter over time. Design every feature around daily, weekly, and monthly engagement. Build monetization only after retention is established. Think like CRED, Notion, and Duolingo combined for personal finance, while keeping the architecture modular, privacy-first, DPDP-compliant, and scalable."*

This statement governs every decision in this suite. When a feature idea conflicts with it, the vision wins.

## The core distinction: AI CFO, not AI Assistant
This is the moat. Most products build an **assistant**. We build a **CFO**.

| AI **Assistant** | AI **CFO** (BudgetKatta) |
|------------------|--------------------------|
| Answers questions | **Plans** ahead |
| Forgets each chat | **Remembers** everything (salary, EMIs, goals, risk) |
| Reactive | **Tracks** continuously |
| You ask, it replies | **Reminds** you before it matters |
| No sense of progress | **Measures** health over time |
| Generic advice | **Improves** your specific situation |

An assistant is a search box with manners. A CFO is a relationship with your money. Retention, trust, and revenue all follow from being the CFO.

## The organizing principle: people solve life events, not buy products
Nobody wakes up wanting a "term insurance policy." They want to **protect their new baby.** Nobody wants an "FD" — they want an **emergency cushion before marriage.** The OS is organized around **life events**, and every financial product surfaces *only* as the answer to the event the user is actually living.

```
Life Timeline (the spine of the OS)

 Age 23 ── First Salary ── Emergency Fund ── Health Insurance ── SIP
     └── Marriage ── Home Purchase ── Car ── Baby ── Education ── Retirement
```
The **Life Events Engine** (feature F11) reads where the user is on this timeline and drives the Score, goals, nudges, reminders, and contextual recommendations. This is what makes the OS feel alive rather than a set of tools.

## Why the old (comparison) model caps out
The audit confirmed it: comparison traffic answers one query and churns; revenue depends on catching intent once, in a slice owned by BankBazaar/Groww; no first-party data → no memory → no moat. An OS inverts all three: it holds the relationship, accumulates memory, and compounds.

## The retention thesis (unchanged, now sharper)
> If a user checks their **Financial Health Score** weekly and lets the **AI CFO** act on one thing a month, everything else — affiliate revenue, premium conversion, referrals — follows. Engagement leads; revenue lags, deliberately.

The Score is the **hook**. The Life Timeline + goals + reminders + monthly AI report are the **habit**. The **AI Memory** makes it smarter each visit (compounding switching cost). Contextual recommendations are the **quiet monetization** that rides on earned trust.

Think **CRED × Notion × Duolingo** for money: CRED's trust-and-delight, Notion's "my whole life lives here" retention, Duolingo's streak-and-progress habit engine.

## North-Star Metric
**Weekly Active Members who took a financial action (WAMa).**
A "financial action" = updated profile/memory, logged a goal contribution, advanced a life-event stage, opened the monthly report, completed an AI action, or re-checked the Score. Impossible to inflate with vanity traffic; directly predicts retention and revenue.

### Metric tree
```
★ North Star: Weekly Active Members w/ an action (WAMa)
├── Acquisition   → signups (Google + OTP completion)
├── Activation    → profile + first Score + first life-event mapped (D0)   ≥ 55%
├── Retention     → D1/D7/D30 · weekly Score re-check                       ≥ 30/18% · 40%
├── Engagement    → goals · nudges done · AI actions run · report opens     ≥ 45% opens
├── Intelligence  → memory facts stored/user (proxy for "smarter over time")
├── Referral      → invites · family members added · K-factor
└── Revenue (lags)→ Plus MRR · contextual-rec fit-CTR · advisor bookings    2–4% · ≥6%
```
`Intelligence` is a deliberately new branch: an OS that remembers should show memory *growing* per user — that's the moat becoming measurable.

## Retention loops (now five + the timeline)
| Loop | Cadence | Trigger | Payoff |
|------|---------|---------|--------|
| **Score** | Weekly | "Your score can rise 40 pts" | A rising number |
| **Goal** | Weekly/Monthly | "Log this month's SIP" | Visible progress |
| **Report** | Monthly | AI CFO monthly plan ready | Personal insight + 1 action |

## Positioning & moat, stacked
1. **In-language + local** (Marathi-first, Maharashtra, co-op-aware) — incumbents can't copy quickly.
2. **First-party financial memory** — the CFO remembers; a comparison site can't.
3. **Life-event organization** — solves the user's actual moment, not a product SKU.
4. **Family as the network** — one household, many members → retention + virality.
Together these compound into a moat no English-first national player has.

## Guardrail: monetization is second, on purpose
Build the contextual engine early but **gate its aggressiveness behind retention health.** If D7 < 25%, tune the habit loop, not ad density. The CFO must never sell against the user's interest — that trust is the durable asset the whole OS rests on.
